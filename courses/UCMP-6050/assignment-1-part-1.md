## 1. Problem Exploration

### Topic 1: Challenges in Sustaining Regular Physical Activity in Graduate Student Life

As a graduate student who values swimming and regular exercise, I am interested in why maintaining physical activity becomes difficult when academic life is busy and unpredictable. Graduate students often face coursework, research tasks, meetings, deadlines, and constantly changing schedules. Even when good sports facilities are available, exercise may still be postponed because of fatigue, fragmented time, competing priorities, or difficulty maintaining motivation. Therefore, the challenge may involve more than simply a lack of time or access to facilities. There may be a gap between students’ intention to stay active and what their academic routines realistically allow them to sustain. This problem matters because regular physical activity affects not only physical health, but also energy, stress management, wellbeing, and the overall quality of graduate student life.

### Topic 2: Safety Challenges and Practice Gaps in Industrial Hot-Work Operations

With my background in automation and previous experience working on an industrial safety monitoring project, I am interested in the safety challenges surrounding hot-work operations such as welding and cutting. These activities take place in dynamic environments where workers may face multiple risks involving combustible materials, hazardous gases, equipment conditions, unsafe behavior, and changing surroundings. Although industrial sites usually have safety regulations, supervision procedures, and monitoring measures, gaps may still exist between formal requirements and what actually happens during daily operations. Workers, supervisors, and safety managers may also perceive risks differently and require different types of information. This problem matters because failures in hot-work safety can result in serious injuries, fires, production disruption, and significant organizational losses. Understanding where current safety practices remain insufficient is therefore an important problem space to explore.

## 2. Stakeholder Identification

### 2.1 Selected Problem Space

I selected “Challenges in Sustaining Regular Physical Activity in Graduate Student Life” for further exploration. This problem space is closely connected to graduate students’ everyday experience and provides access to relevant stakeholders for interviews and observations. I am particularly interested in understanding why students who intend to exercise regularly may still struggle to sustain physical activity under changing academic schedules and competing demands. At this stage, I do not assume that lack of time is the main cause; different behavioral, social, environmental, and institutional factors may be relevant and require further investigation.

### 2.2 Stakeholder Mapping — Power-Interest Matrix

| Power / Interest | Low Interest | High Interest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **High Power** | Academic Supervisors / Program Administrators | Sports Center / Facility Managers; Student Wellbeing / Student Affairs Staff |
| **Low Power** | General Peers / Roommates not actively involved in exercise | Graduate Students with Irregular Exercise Habits; Graduate Students with Regular Exercise Habits; Sports Clubs / Student Organizers |

Graduate students are the primary stakeholders because they directly experience the challenge. Students with irregular exercise habits may reveal barriers and points where intended exercise breaks down, while regular exercisers provide useful contrasting experiences. Sports center managers influence facility access, opening arrangements, booking procedures, and activity provision, while student wellbeing staff may influence broader student-support initiatives. Academic supervisors and program administrators have an indirect but potentially important influence because research tasks, meetings, deadlines, and academic expectations shape students’ schedules and priorities. Sports clubs may affect participation through social support and shared activities. These relationships suggest that sustaining physical activity may be influenced by interactions among individual routines, academic demands, social support, and the campus environment. Graduate students and sports-related staff have relatively high interest because the issue directly affects their exercise experience or service responsibilities, whereas academic stakeholders have greater influence but lower direct interest because physical activity is not their primary responsibility.

**Engagement priority:** graduate students with irregular exercise habits, followed by regular exercisers and sports center managers, because these groups can provide direct and contrasting perspectives on the problem.

## 3. Investigation Plan

### 3.1 Questions

- **Q1.** What situations or factors most often cause graduate students to postpone, shorten, or cancel planned exercise?
- **Q2.** How do academic demands, daily routines, personal motivation, and environmental conditions interact to shape graduate students’ exercise routines?
- **Q3.** What differences exist between graduate students who maintain regular exercise habits and those who struggle to do so?
- **Q4.** How well do current campus sports facilities, opening hours, booking procedures, and organized activities fit graduate students’ actual routines and needs?

### 3.2 Methods for Verification and Information Gathering

**Interviews and Observations:** Conduct semi-structured interviews with graduate students who have different exercise habits, especially irregular and regular exercisers. Questions will focus on specific experiences, such as the most recent time they planned to exercise but postponed or cancelled it, what influenced that decision, and what makes exercise easier or more difficult to maintain. Selected campus sports facilities, such as the swimming pool or gym, will also be observed at different times of day to record crowd levels, waiting conditions, usage patterns, and possible difficulties related to access or facility use.

**Literature Review:** Review relevant academic literature, reports, and case studies on physical activity among university students, barriers to regular exercise, exercise adherence, academic workload, time pressure, and student wellbeing.

**Product / Service Analysis:** Review current campus sports-related services, including facility opening hours, booking procedures, available facilities, organized activities, and relevant information channels, with attention to possible gaps between existing services and graduate students’ actual routines and needs.
